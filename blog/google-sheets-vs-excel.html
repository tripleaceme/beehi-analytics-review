Google Sheets vs Excel for Small Business: How to Choose

Spreadsheet guidesBy BeeHi AnalyticsPublished 25 March 2026Updated 25 September 20265 min read

The takeaway

Choose the tool that fits how your team works, then maintain consistent records. Useful analytics depends more on reliable information than the file format.

Choose Google Sheets when a shared browser-based record fits your team’s routine. Choose Excel when your existing workbooks, desktop workflow or Microsoft tools matter more. Both can support collaboration and dashboard reporting. The right choice depends on how you enter records, work offline and review information.

For a small business in Nigeria, test the tasks your staff actually perform: entering a sale on a phone, correcting a payment, working during an internet outage and finding last month’s totals. A feature list is less useful than a tool your team can use consistently.

1. Collaboration: both can work with a team

The old comparison that “Excel means emailing attachments” is incomplete. Microsoft supports Excel co-authoring when the workbook and participants meet its requirements, including appropriate OneDrive or SharePoint storage.

Whichever tool you choose, nominate one working file. Sending separate copies through email or WhatsApp creates competing versions. Give staff edit access only where needed, protect formula areas and agree who corrects mistakes.

Try this: have two staff members enter separate sample transactions, then ask the owner to find both. Check that nobody has to merge two files manually.

2. Offline work: plan around your connection

Excel desktop is useful when local workbook editing is central to your work. Shared cloud changes still need to synchronise when you reconnect. Google also supports offline work in Sheets on supported setups, but prepare access before the connection disappears.

Test on the actual phone or computer your business uses. A file working offline on one laptop does not prove every staff member can open it offline. Agree how to handle conflicting edits after an outage.

3. Phone use: test the entry screen, not just the app

Both products have mobile options. A sheet with 40 columns can still be difficult to use on a small screen. Put essential entry fields first, use short labels and keep reports separate from the input table.

A salon receptionist might need Date, Service, Amount, Payment Method and Staff Member. The owner might only need totals and trends. They do not need identical screens to work from the same records.

4. Dashboards: neither format is the only option

Google Sheets is not the only spreadsheet that can feed a dashboard. For example, Microsoft documents Power BI dashboards linked to Excel workbooks on OneDrive. The reporting tool and connection method determine what is possible.

Ask how updates reach the report, how often it refreshes, what happens when a column changes and who maintains the connection. A cloud file does not automatically mean every dashboard updates instantly. BeeHi Analytics supports Google Sheets and Microsoft Excel as data sources, with connection and refresh arrangements agreed during setup.

5. Formulas and existing workbooks

Everyday totals, conditional calculations and summaries are available in both tools. But a complex workbook may depend on features, automation or integrations that do not transfer unchanged. Do not migrate a working business system just because another tool looks simpler.

Make a copy, test representative records and compare totals before switching. Review dates, formulas, dropdowns and permissions. Keep the original until the new process has been checked and staff know where to work.

6. Cost includes the work around the software

Compare the account, storage, administration and reporting requirements your business needs. Check current regional plans rather than relying on an old monthly naira figure. A software price does not include the time needed to organise records, repair formulas or build and maintain a dashboard.

Ask for a clear breakdown of one-time setup, ongoing subscriptions and optional support. Avoid paying for a migration if a simpler correction to your existing sheet solves the problem.

A practical choice for three common situations

  • A laundry starting a shared order log: trial Sheets if staff are comfortable opening a shared Google file. Test phone entry and connection reliability.
  • A retailer with established Excel workbooks: inspect the existing formulas and cloud-sharing options before rebuilding anything.
  • An owner who only wants to check performance: choose the reporting view after confirming how staff will keep the underlying records accurate.

Before deciding, list your five most common tasks and run them in a test file. Note time taken, errors and access problems. Keep the tool that supports your working day with the least unnecessary change.

Common questions about Sheets and Excel

Can Excel connect to a dashboard?

Yes. Supported reporting tools can use Excel data. File storage, connector support and refresh configuration determine the workflow.

Do I need to switch tools to improve my records?

Often, no. Consistent dates, unique transaction IDs and clear categories may solve the immediate problem. See our expense tracking setup for a concrete example of a simple structure.

How to Track Business Expenses in Google Sheets

Practical tutorialsBy BeeHi AnalyticsPublished 1 April 2026Updated 25 September 20265 min read

The takeaway

Use consistent categories and dates, record each expense once and reconcile totals before drawing conclusions about business costs.

To track business expenses in Google Sheets, create one Expenses tab with a row for each transaction, use consistent categories and build a separate monthly summary. Record amounts as numbers and dates as dates so the totals can be calculated reliably.

This guide shows a simple record of business cash outflows. It helps you see what you paid and where the money went. It is not, on its own, a calculation of accounting profit: stock purchases, equipment and loan repayments can require different treatment in your accounts.

1. Create the Expenses tab

Create a spreadsheet and rename its first tab Expenses. Use these headers in row 1, in this exact order, so the formulas below match your columns:

Format column A as Date and column D as your chosen currency. Enter 12000, not text such as “₦12,000 paid”. Use the same currency throughout this simple tracker; mixed currencies should not be added without a consistent conversion method.

Freeze the header row and keep one continuous table. Do not insert subtotal rows between transactions, because later totals could count those amounts twice. Keep charts and summaries on another tab.

2. Choose categories your business can maintain

Start with a manageable list: Materials or Stock, Rent, Wages, Transport, Power, Internet, Marketing, Packaging, Equipment and Other. Adjust it to your business rather than creating a separate category for every supplier.

Use a dropdown in column C to prevent “Transport”, “transport” and “Delivery transport” from becoming separate groups. Google’s dropdown instructions show how to create and edit the list. Keep category names stable once the sheet is in use.

Keep transfers between your own accounts, money introduced by the owner and personal withdrawals separate from ordinary operating expenses. A change in bank balance is not always a business cost.

3. Enter a few records and check the structure

Use a copy of the sheet to try these illustrative September entries:

  • 2 September: Packaging, ₦12,000.
  • 3 September: Transport, ₦8,000.
  • 5 September: Packaging, ₦6,000.
  • 1 October: Packaging, ₦10,000.

September should total ₦26,000, with ₦18,000 for Packaging. The October entry should not appear in September’s summary. Delete the sample rows before entering your real records.

Record expenses close to when they happen, keep supporting references and agree who enters which payments. If two people record the same transfer, a correct formula still gives an incorrect total.

4. Calculate the monthly total

Create a tab named Summary. Put a real month-start date, such as 1 September 2026, in B1. Put this formula in B3:

=SUMIFS(Expenses!D2:D1000,Expenses!A2:A1000,">="&$B$1,Expenses!A2:A1000,"<"&EDATE($B$1,1))

This adds amounts from the month’s first day up to, but not including, the next month’s first day. The upper boundary also handles entries that include a time on the last day. Change B1 to review a different month.

The examples cover rows 2–1000. Extend every matching range if you add more records. Some spreadsheet locales use semicolons rather than commas; keep dates as date values instead of typing date strings into the formula. See Google’s SUMIFS reference.

5. Calculate spending by category for the same month

On Summary, put Category in A5 and Total in B5. Enter Packaging in A6, Transport in A7 and your remaining categories below. Put this in B6 and copy down:

=SUMIFS(Expenses!D2:D1000,Expenses!C2:C1000,A6,Expenses!A2:A1000,">="&$B$1,Expenses!A2:A1000,"<"&EDATE($B$1,1))

The date conditions matter. A category total without them would show all recorded months, which could be mistaken for this month’s spending. Check that the category totals add up to the monthly total; differences can reveal blank or unexpected categories.

6. Reconcile and review the figures

Compare recorded transfers with the bank statement and recorded cash payments with the cash record. Check duplicate references, missing receipts and dates stored as text. Investigate differences rather than adding an unexplained adjustment to force a match.

Then ask what changed: did transport increase because there were more deliveries, or did the cost per delivery rise? Did you buy materials for future orders? A larger monthly outflow is not automatically waste.

A category chart can make patterns easier to see. If you also compare spending with sales, use the same reporting period and clear definitions. Do not call the difference “net profit” unless the relevant costs and accounting adjustments are included.

When to add a dashboard

Start with records you trust. If you regularly rebuild the same summaries, a dashboard can help display category totals and trends. It will not correct duplicate entries or missing amounts by itself. See when a business dashboard is worth considering and how Sheets compares with Excel.

Common questions about expense tracking

Should I make a new tab each month?

For this setup, keep transactions in one Expenses tab and change the month in Summary. This makes comparisons easier and avoids maintaining twelve versions of a formula.

What if I do not have a receipt?

Record what you know and flag the missing evidence. Keep the payment reference and follow up for the supporting document rather than inventing details.

Can I use this as my full accounting system?

It is a basic payment tracker. Reporting profit, tax or other formal accounts can require additional records and professional accounting support.
